Source

Alberta's Restructured Energy Market replaces the single pool price with locational marginal prices: generators are paid the price at their own node, while most loads pay a uniform Alberta Load Price. This proof of concept shows what analytics and AI add to the two jobs that change creates — understanding and forecasting congestion, and making sure the models behind interconnection studies are right — using AESO's public 2025 area-level backcast and planning-area load and generation.

      Pricing hubs: predefined regions vs data-driven clusters

      Hubs give the market liquid reference prices for hedging. AESO judges a design on two numbers: basis deviation (how far member prices sit from their hub, lower is better) and minimum separation (the smallest gap between any two hub prices, higher is clearer). Clusters are Ward linkage on hourly price series, restricted to neighbouring areas so each hub is one connected region, with at least three areas per hub.

      Day-ahead basis forecast with calibrated uncertainty

      One LightGBM model across all 42 areas predicts tomorrow's basis from conditions (load, imports, regional net generation, the system price) and recent prices. Quantile models give 80% bands, recalibrated each day on the last 30 settled days (conformal prediction). Trained Jan–Aug, calibrated Sep, tested on Oct–Dec, the fall outage season. Conditions are 2025 actuals standing in for the forecasts a production model would use, so scores are an upper bound.

      Skill by area

      Test-period error, band coverage and separation AUC per planning area.

      A transparent SCED: prices straight from the duals

      DC optimal power flow with N-1 security and losses, solved as a linear program (HiGHS). LMP = MCE (energy at the reference bus) + MCL (losses) + MCC (congestion), read from the shadow prices of the balance and flow constraints, then checked against brute force: re-dispatch with one more MW at the bus.

      PDUP model validation: a structured first-pass review

      A reviewer's checklist as code: 30 rules over data, topology, lines, transformers, generators and dynamic models, then an AC power flow, short-circuit ratio, sensitivity and an anomaly model trained on accepted models. Two synthetic submissions: a wind farm with typical data errors and a clean solar-plus-storage project.

      Agentic analyst: tools, not guesses

      A local LLM plans tool calls over the warehouse, the forecast, the SCED engine and the PDUP reviews, then writes the answer from what the tools return. Every number in an answer is traced back to a tool result.

      Tools

        Guardrails

        • Local only. The model runs in LM Studio on this machine; the client refuses non-local endpoints.
        • Read-only SQL. One SELECT, read-only connection, file access off, 50-row cap.
        • Verified facts. Each tool returns pre-written sentences next to its raw fields; small models paraphrase far more reliably than they read tables.
        • Number tracing. Every figure in the answer is matched to a tool result; untraced figures are flagged.
        • Offline fallback. Without a model server, a deterministic planner answers the same questions.

          Data, method and limits

          What is real, what is synthetic, and what a production version would need.

          AESO public data used

            AESO data are used under AESO's terms of use; they are downloaded by lmplab fetch and never committed to the repository.

            Honest limits

            • Area level, not nodal. The backcast gives one LMP per planning area; AESO's own hub analysis uses bus-level prices, so basis deviations here run higher.
            • Forecast inputs are actuals. Load, regional generation and the system price are 2025 actuals standing in for the forecasts a production model would use, so scores are an upper bound.
            • Surplus is not congestion rent. The uniform-price surplus includes loss rent and is computed at area level; AESO's $91M congestion rent is the comparable published figure.
            • Stylized network. The seven-zone SCED studies are illustrative, tuned to behave like Alberta's corridors, not AESO's PSS/E model.
            • Synthetic PDUPs. Both submissions, the screening ranges and the accepted-model library are invented for the demonstration; real reviews follow the AESO PDUP checklist and ISO rules.
            • YAML, not PSS/E files. Submissions are YAML stand-ins for PSS/E RAW/DYR files and AESO data forms; a production version would read the case files directly and run AESO's dynamic model performance test bench.
